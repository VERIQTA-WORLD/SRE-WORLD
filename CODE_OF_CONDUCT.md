# SRE World Code of Conduct

## Our Commitment

SRE World is committed to providing a respectful, professional, inclusive, and safe environment for everyone who participates in the project.

We welcome people with different levels of experience, identities, backgrounds, locations, languages, disciplines, organizations, and technical perspectives.

Participation should remain free from harassment, discrimination, intimidation, personal attacks, and retaliation.

This commitment applies to contributors, maintainers, reviewers, issue reporters, community members, speakers, partners, and anyone representing SRE World or VERIQTA.

---

## Our Purpose

SRE World exists to collect, create, review, and share high-quality Site Reliability Engineering knowledge.

The community should help people:

- Understand reliability engineering
- Evaluate technical evidence
- Learn from production failure
- Discuss operational risk honestly
- Improve systems without blaming individuals
- Contribute practical and safe engineering knowledge
- Develop professional judgment

Technical disagreement is expected. Disrespectful conduct is not.

---

## Expected Behavior

Community members are expected to:

- Communicate respectfully and professionally.
- Welcome questions asked in good faith.
- Critique ideas, evidence, code, and documentation, not people.
- Explain technical disagreement clearly.
- Support important claims with evidence where possible.
- Acknowledge uncertainty and correct mistakes openly.
- Respect different experience levels and areas of expertise.
- Give contributors reasonable time to respond to feedback.
- Keep discussions relevant to the repository and contribution.
- Protect private, confidential, and personally identifying information.
- Disclose relevant commercial or professional relationships.
- Follow the repository’s contribution, resource, safety, and licensing standards.
- Accept maintainer decisions when they are made in good faith under project policy.
- Report harmful conduct responsibly.
- Help maintain a community where people can participate without fear of humiliation or retaliation.

---

## Constructive Technical Disagreement

SRE involves uncertainty, tradeoffs, incomplete evidence, and different operating environments. Two experienced engineers may reach different conclusions without either person acting in bad faith.

Constructive disagreement should:

- Identify the exact claim, decision, or change under discussion.
- Explain the operating context and assumptions.
- Provide evidence, examples, or authoritative references.
- Separate confirmed facts from interpretation.
- State risks and tradeoffs.
- Identify what evidence could change the conclusion.
- Suggest a correction or alternative when possible.

Examples of constructive language:

```text
This recommendation may increase the blast radius because...
```

```text
The calculation appears to use requests rather than valid events. Could we verify the denominator?
```

```text
This approach may be appropriate for one environment, but it should not be presented as universal because...
```

```text
I found a primary source that differs from the current description.
```

Avoid:

```text
Anyone who believes this is incompetent.
```

```text
This is stupid.
```

```text
You clearly do not understand SRE.
```

```text
Real engineers already know this.
```

Maintainers may close repetitive, hostile, or unproductive arguments after the technical issue has been addressed.

## Learning From Failure Without Blame

SRE World applies the same principle to community discussions that mature reliability teams apply to incidents.

When reviewing mistakes:

- Examine the conditions that made the mistake possible.
- Consider missing context, unclear ownership, weak controls, and system design.
- Explain why an action may have appeared reasonable at the time.
- Focus on improving documentation, review, testing, and safeguards.
- Preserve accountability without humiliation.

Blamelessness does not mean that all behavior is acceptable. Deliberate misconduct, deception, harassment, privacy violations, and repeated disregard for safety may result in enforcement action.

## Unacceptable Behavior

Unacceptable behavior includes:

- Harassment, discrimination, or intimidation
- Personal attacks, insults, or degrading comments
- Threats of violence or encouragement of harm
- Sexual attention, sexualized language, or sexual imagery directed at another participant
- Deliberate misgendering or use of rejected names
- Mocking someone’s language, accent, education, location, or experience level
- Public or private bullying
- Trolling, baiting, or deliberately inflammatory comments
- Repeated disruption of issues, discussions, or reviews
- Following someone across platforms to continue an unwanted interaction
- Publishing or threatening to publish private information
- Sharing private messages without a valid safety or legal reason
- Revealing personal data, credentials, employer information, or confidential incident details
- Impersonating another person or organization
- Knowingly submitting fabricated sources, incidents, benchmarks, or technical evidence
- Plagiarism or deliberate copyright violation
- Undisclosed advertising, affiliate promotion, or coordinated spam
- Manipulating votes, reactions, stars, or contributor recognition
- Retaliating against someone who reports misconduct or raises a safety concern
- Encouraging unsafe or unauthorized access to systems
- Attempting to exploit project infrastructure or community members
- Any conduct that would reasonably make another person fear for their safety

This list is not exhaustive. Maintainers may act on harmful behavior not specifically listed here.

---

## Harassment

Harassment includes repeated or severe unwelcome behavior that targets or intimidates another person.

It may occur through:

- Public issues
- Pull requests
- Reviews
- Discussions
- Commit messages
- Email
- Social media
- Video or voice calls
- Direct messages
- Community events
- Other spaces connected to the project

If someone clearly asks you to stop contacting them about a non-essential matter, stop.

Project communication required for moderation, security, legal compliance, or completion of an active contribution may continue through an appropriate official channel.

---

## Privacy and Confidential Information

Do not publish:

- Passwords, tokens, private keys, or credentials
- Private email addresses, telephone numbers, or home addresses
- Customer data
- Private production logs
- Internal company documents
- Confidential incident information
- Security vulnerabilities before responsible disclosure
- Private conversations without permission
- Personally identifying information that is not already intentionally public and relevant

If sensitive information is posted accidentally:

1. Do not quote, copy, or redistribute it.
2. Notify a maintainer privately.
3. If credentials are involved, advise the owner to revoke or rotate them immediately.
4. Allow maintainers to remove the content and preserve only the minimum evidence required.

Removing a secret from Git history does not make the secret safe. Exposed credentials must be revoked or rotated.

---

## Responsible Security Reporting

Do not publish an exploitable security vulnerability in a public issue when doing so could expose users, contributors, or project infrastructure to harm.

Use GitHub private vulnerability reporting when it is available. If it is unavailable, contact the maintainers privately at:

```text
veriqta@gmail.com
```

Include:

- A clear description of the issue
- The affected location or component
- Steps to reproduce, when safe
- Potential impact
- Suggested mitigation, if known
- Whether the information has been shared elsewhere

Do not:

- Access data that does not belong to you
- Disrupt project services
- Use destructive testing
- Attempt social engineering
- Demand payment or threaten disclosure
- Publish private vulnerability details before maintainers have had a reasonable opportunity to respond

Good-faith security research does not excuse privacy violations, unauthorized access, extortion, or harmful testing.

## Reporting Misconduct

Report conduct concerns privately to:

```text
veriqta@gmail.com
```

Use a clear subject line:

```text
SRE World Code of Conduct Report
```

Include as much of the following as you can safely provide:

- Your preferred name
- Your contact method
- The name or username of the person involved
- Where the behavior occurred
- Date and approximate time
- Description of what happened
- Links, screenshots, or other evidence
- Whether the behavior is continuing
- Whether anyone else witnessed it
- Immediate safety concerns
- The outcome you are requesting, if any

You may omit information that would place you at additional risk.

Do not file a public issue containing sensitive allegations, private evidence, or personally identifying information.

---

## Confidentiality of Reports

Maintainers will limit access to a report to the people necessary to evaluate and address it.

They may need to share limited information when:

- Another person must respond to an allegation
- A platform must enforce its policies
- Legal or safety obligations apply
- Immediate harm must be prevented

Maintainers will avoid unnecessary disclosure and will not promise absolute confidentiality when safety or legal obligations make that impossible.

Anonymous reports may be considered, but limited information can make investigation and follow-up more difficult.

---

## Protection Against Retaliation

Retaliation is prohibited.

Retaliation includes punishing, threatening, harassing, exposing, excluding, or targeting someone because they:

- Reported misconduct in good faith
- Participated in an investigation
- Supported another participant
- Raised a security or privacy concern
- Challenged discriminatory or unsafe conduct

A report made in good faith will not violate this Code of Conduct merely because the concern cannot be confirmed.

Knowingly false or malicious reports may themselves result in enforcement action.

---

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers are responsible for:

- Clarifying expected behavior
- Receiving and reviewing reports
- Protecting sensitive information
- Considering available evidence fairly
- Addressing conflicts of interest
- Taking proportionate action
- Documenting material enforcement decisions privately
- Communicating outcomes when appropriate
- Protecting reporters and participants from retaliation
- Correcting moderation mistakes when new evidence becomes available

Maintainers may remove, edit, hide, reject, or close comments, commits, issues, pull requests, discussions, and other contributions that violate this Code of Conduct or project policies.

Maintainers may also restrict or remove a participant’s access to project spaces.

---

## Conflicts of Interest

A maintainer should not lead the review of a report when they:

- Are the subject of the report
- Have a close personal or professional relationship with a person involved
- Have a financial interest in the outcome
- Were directly involved in the disputed event
- Cannot evaluate the matter impartially

When possible, another maintainer or trusted reviewer should handle the matter.

If the report concerns the only available maintainer, the reporter may also use GitHub’s abuse-reporting or safety channels.

---

## Enforcement Guidelines

Maintainers will consider:

- Severity of the behavior
- Actual and potential harm
- Whether the behavior was deliberate
- Whether it was repeated
- Whether the person understood the expectation
- Response to feedback
- Relevant prior conduct
- Risk to the community
- Need to protect affected participants

The following levels guide enforcement. Maintainers may skip levels when severity or safety requires it.

### Level 1: Informal Correction

**Community impact:** Minor unprofessional behavior with limited impact.

**Possible action:** A private or public reminder, clarification, edit request, or redirection.

**Expected result:** The behavior stops and the discussion returns to a professional standard.

### Level 2: Formal Warning

**Community impact:** A clear violation, repeated disruption, disrespectful conduct, or failure to respond to an earlier correction.

**Possible action:** A written warning describing the violation, required behavior, and consequences of repetition. Related content may be removed or hidden.

**Expected result:** Immediate correction and no retaliation.

### Level 3: Temporary Restriction

**Community impact:** Serious or repeated violation, harassment, targeted hostility, privacy risk, or continued misconduct after warning.

**Possible action:** Temporary restriction from issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews, community channels, or other project participation.

Conditions for return may be specified.

### Level 4: Permanent Removal

**Community impact:** Severe harassment, credible threats, deliberate exposure of private information, malicious security activity, sustained abuse, retaliation, or conduct showing continued risk to the community.

**Possible action:** Permanent removal from project participation and official community spaces. Relevant platforms may also receive a report.

### Immediate Protective Action

Maintainers may take immediate temporary action before completing a review when necessary to:

- Stop ongoing harassment
- Remove secrets or private information
- Prevent security harm
- Protect a participant from credible threats
- Preserve the integrity of the project

Temporary action is not automatically a final finding.



The request should identify:

- New evidence
- A conflict of interest

Disagreement with the outcome alone is not evidence of unfair review.

The reviewing maintainer may uphold, modify, or reverse the decision. Repeated requests without new information may receive no further response.

## Good-Faith Moderation

Moderation decisions are made to protect community safety, participation, and project integrity.

Moderators should not use enforcement to:

- Silence respectful technical disagreement
- Protect inaccurate content from evidence-based correction
- Favor contributors because of status or popularity
- Punish personal criticism of a technical decision when expressed professionally
- Promote a commercial interest

People may challenge a technical decision without violating this Code of Conduct. The manner, persistence, relevance, and impact of the communication still matter.

---

## Maintainer Communication

When appropriate and safe, maintainers should communicate:

- That a report was received
- Whether additional information is needed
- Whether immediate protective action was taken
- When the review is complete
- Whether action was taken

Maintainers may not disclose private details, evidence, or consequences to people who are not directly involved.

Public statements may be issued when necessary to correct misinformation, protect the community, or explain a visible project action. Such statements should reveal the minimum necessary information.

---

## Project Decisions and Moderation

Not every disagreement is a Code of Conduct matter.

Maintainers may reject or close a contribution because it:

- Falls outside the SRE World scope
- Does not meet the Resource Standard
- Lacks evidence
- Duplicates existing material
- Creates maintenance burden
- Introduces safety or licensing concerns
- Conflicts with the repository structure

A content decision does not automatically imply misconduct by the contributor.

Similarly, disagreement with a maintainer decision does not automatically constitute harassment. It becomes a conduct matter when communication violates this policy.
